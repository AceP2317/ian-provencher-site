On my subscription plan, Claude's usage service reports three windows: a 5-hour window, a 7-day window, and a weekly window scoped to one model. That plan is Ultra, and Anthropic [removes the 5-hour window once it verifies the account](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17728952-what-is-the-ultra-plan); the strip draws a window only while the service reports one. Each fills as you work and resets on its own clock. The HUD is the strip I keep above the prompt to see all three, plus the prompt cache and the context, without asking.

The part worth explaining is the grading. A bar that turns amber once most of a window is gone says nothing useful on day six of seven, and it says too little on the first morning. So nothing on the strip is graded on how much is gone. Each window is graded on where it will land at its reset, and the week is graded against a plan that is deliberately front-loaded.

The other two posts in this series cover the planning half, [Plan mode, enforced](/blog/intent-lock/), and the proof half, [The completion meter](/blog/completion-meter/). The [workflow post](/blog/plan-to-proof/) traces one job through all of it.

## What it does not do

It does not know my plan's allowance in tokens. Every figure is a percent of a window, as Claude's usage service reports it, and that is all it needs: the grade is about the shape of spending inside a window, not its size.

It does not predict a burst. The recent-rate landing reacts to one once it has ten minutes of rising readings, and the plan landing never reacts to one at all. The strip tells me where I am heading, and the decision to slow down stays mine.

It does not hide its own faults. When the usage reading fails, the strip adds the error to the line, and a window whose reading has gone stale shows a question mark rather than its last figure.

## Where the code lives

The strip and its core live in my private setup repository, so this post quotes them rather than linking them. The strip has its own test suite. The core is also the VS Code sidebar's, and the sidebar's installer runs eleven suites before it installs anything, two of them built to drive the moments live data rarely shows: a closed weekend, and the instant the weekend closes.
