An S-1 is a supplier's audited financials, released to everyone at once. For a planner, the habit is the same one you already use with a critical supplier: before you commit to a long contract, read the supplier's debt schedule and what it owes next year. Anthropic's filing is where that kind of information should appear, for a company many of your clients will depend on. I have not opened it yet, so this is a pointer, not a finding. You will not have to ask for it. You will have to read the risk factors, because that is where a company says what could go wrong.

The debt items are the same lesson at a different layer. Data center capacity is a supply line, and whoever funds it carries the financing risk. When a bank package stalls, the capacity behind it can be delayed or re-priced, and the buyer may be the last to know. For a planning job, the useful question is not only what a unit of compute costs, but who funds the capacity you are buying and what their debt looks like this quarter.

The chip items are a supply-certainty question. If a chip can be found in a network it was not licensed for, and the rules for it keep moving, then the lead time and the availability of the hardware behind a cloud price are not fixed. You do not control export rules. You can control how many of your AI commitments depend on one vendor's allocation of one chip family.

The practical step this week is small, and it fits on one line of your vendor review. Next to each AI vendor you depend on, write two things: who funds the capacity behind the service, and what export or supply exposure its hardware carries. If you cannot fill in either for a vendor your work depends on, that is where the surprise is most likely to come from.
