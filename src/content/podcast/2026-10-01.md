Before we close, the AppliedIQ angle.

Put the day side by side. A frontier model goes to a list, and its maker says it is in a government pre-release process whose standards, per CBS, are not public. Micron says most of its 2027 output is committed, at an eighty-seven percent company-wide margin. Oracle bills agent work by the meter. Anthropic offers government buyers a hard cap and, the same day, tells developers one model retires in sixty-one days. The common thread, in your language, is that the price of a capability is no longer the price on the quote. It is the credential, the meter, the commodity input, and the notice period. Every one of those can move after you sign.

In your world, that is a price-escalation clause. Buyers of copper and resin have always put one in, because a fixed quote on a variable input is a bet you did not mean to place. So the angle for AppliedIQ is to write those moving parts into the offer, so the client sees them and you are not the one holding them.

One concrete action, and two quick ones.

The action: build the offer as a hard cap with named exceptions. Cap the cost of the agent's work at a number the client chooses, the way Claude for Government is bought, in fixed increments under a hard ceiling, and say in the offer what happens at the cap: the agent stops and asks a named person, not an automated reply, which is yesterday's lesson. Then write three pass-through lines. Model retirement: you migrate the client's automation when a vendor retires a model, billed as a maintenance line. Hardware: any quote that includes a box with memory in it is valid for thirty days and carries a pass-through if component prices move, because if Micron's outlook is right, that box keeps repricing. And a dependency line: you do not build a client's workflow on a capability that only a credentialed list can hold. On the third I said that somebody in the six hundred and fifty would hold it and sell you the output. Argon does not change that.

Two quick ones. Check any automation or demo you own for Claude Sonnet 4.5. If it names that model on Anthropic's own API, it stops on the thirtieth of November. That is the kind of line item a client will otherwise discover by an outage. And when a prospect on Oracle asks about Claw, ask them one question: what is the price of one finished task, retries included? That is Nate's question and it is the one the meter cannot answer.

That is my suggestion, not a fact about your market.

Two things to watch. Whether Argon shows up on Google's models page for everyone, which is today's call. And whether any U.S. agency or lab publishes what the pre-release process measures. As far as I could find, none has, and until one does, every phased rollout will be a delay you cannot read.
