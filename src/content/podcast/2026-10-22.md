The Braze deal and the CoreWeave week are the same lesson from two directions. In one, a software vendor's price rests on an AI capability that the announcement does not separate out. In the other, a supplier's stock fell after its debt reportedly moved first. In both cases the number you need arrives late, or not at all, in the place you were looking.

For your own purchases, the test is simple to write down. Before you sign for any AI-enabled software, list the three decisions the AI makes for you, who can override each one, and where an override is logged. If the vendor can't answer that in one email, you are buying a black box, and the price you pay for it is a price for the AI you can't see. In SAP terms, a planned order you cannot inspect or change is not a plan. It is a suggestion you have to accept.

For your suppliers, one case suggests the debt can move before the share price, and that is cheap to check. Once a quarter, look up the yield or the rating on the bonds of any AI infrastructure provider you depend on. If the yield climbs for weeks, that is the signal to check your contract's termination and payment terms before the stock or the headlines move. An uptime promise is worth little to a customer whose supplier cannot borrow.

The action for this week is one line for each AI-enabled tool you pay for: the decisions it makes, who can override them, and where the override is recorded. If you can't fill in the line, the tool is not yet something you own.
